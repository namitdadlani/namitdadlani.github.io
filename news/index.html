OpenAI says its new cybersecurity model is better at not hacking other companies

OpenAI is launching GPT-6 Cyber, a new AI cybersecurity model designed to find system vulnerabilities without rogue hacking. During testing, the model went out of bounds less than 1 percent of the time, down from roughly 5 percent with the previous GPT-5.6 Cyber. Rated as "Critical" on OpenAI's safety framework, GPT-6 Cyber scored 48.5 percent on the ExploitGym benchmark and helped discover 81 previously unknown vulnerabilities in Firefox. OpenAI is initially rolling out the tool to select participants in its Daybreak Red program for authorized defensive work.

Axiom Math wants to keep AI slop out of mathematics

AI startup AxiomMath has officially verified the Stacks Project, a massive 7,654-page open-source mathematical reference started in 2005 by Columbia professor Johan de Jong. Founded in March 2025 by 25-year-old Stanford dropout Carina Hong, the $1.6 billion company deployed autonomous AI agents to translate the entire library into roughly 16 million lines of computer-checked Lean code. The verification effort aims to give AI systems a reliable, bug-free foundation for future science and software engineering. Axiom previously raised a $200 million Series A round led by Menlo Ventures.

Paraguay President Santiago Peña resigns after bank collapse

Paraguayan President Santiago Peña has resigned following a political crisis sparked by the collapse of Ueno Bank. The financial institution entered insolvency this week, freezing operations and preventing millions of citizens from accessing their accounts. Peña held shares until last year in the firm owning the bank, leading opposition lawmakers to initiate impeachment proceedings over alleged conflicts of interest. In a video message, Peña apologized to the public and stated his resignation aims to protect social peace. He originally took office in 2023 representing the conservative Colorado Party.

Ontario court certifies class action against Toronto long-term care home for negligence, COVID-19 deaths

An Ontario court has certified a class-action lawsuit against Toronto's Downsview Long-Term Care Centre and parent company Gem Healthcare Group over their pandemic response. The lawsuit alleges that gross negligence led to multiple COVID-19 outbreaks between late January 2020 and early May 2023, causing at least 1,966 residents to contract the virus and 244 to die. Justice Edward Morgan granted the certification with the consent of the defendants, who intend to fight the claims. Representative plaintiff Billy Powell lost his mother to the home's outbreak in May 2020.

Amazon employees across the Greater Toronto Area have launched a union organizing drive alongside Unifor, Canada's largest private sector union.
