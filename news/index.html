It’s Time to Run an Audit of All the Cloud Storage Links You’ve Shared

Sharing cloud files via Google Drive, OneDrive, iCloud, or Dropbox is convenient, but leaving old links active poses serious security risks. Over time, forgotten links can be forwarded by accident or exposed if a contact gets hacked, giving strangers access to edit or delete your files. Fortunately, auditing your active links only takes a few minutes. While OneDrive and Dropbox let you set expiration dates on shared links, iCloud and Google Drive require manual cleanups.

The OpenAI board member who fired Sam Altman says we still can’t trust AI CEOs

Former OpenAI board member Helen Toner warns that relying on tech CEOs to self-regulate AI safety will fail. Beware the New AI-Fueled Ransomware Boom

Artificial intelligence is accelerating a global surge in ransomware attacks by making cybercrime far easier to execute. Researchers recorded nearly 3,000 alleged attacks over the summer, representing up to a 40 percent increase from spring. While AI does not run entire attacks yet, hackers use AI language models to write convincing phishing emails, negotiate ransoms, and launch independent operations. In one case, an inexperienced teenager made over $800,000 in under two weeks. Consequently, experts expect lone-wolf attackers to increasingly favor direct data extortion over traditional file encryption.

FAA expands AI advice to manage air traffic congestion across entire US airspace

The Federal Aviation Administration is expanding its $875 million AI advisory tool, SMART, across all 22 air traffic control centers in the United States. SMART analyzes weather, flight paths, and staffing metrics to recommend optimal routing and scheduling. An initial September test around Washington, D.C., rerouted 180 flights, saving roughly 10 minutes and 80 gallons of fuel per flight. Human controllers retain final authority on whether to accept the recommendations. However, the air traffic controllers' union revealed it was never consulted during the system's design or testing.

Ontario's Bill 140 raises concerns over landfill approvals, groundwater protection in Caledon, Ont.

Ontario’s proposed Bill 140 is sparking concern in Caledon by stripping municipalities of their power to block large landfill projects. Under the legislation, developers no longer need local municipal approval before submitting environmental assessments for new waste sites. Local leaders and environmental groups warn that placing landfills in old aggregate pits could contaminate underground aquifers supplying rural drinking water. The bill also removes citizens' legal rights to request environmental reviews or sue over resource damage. The province defended the bill, stating Ontario has less than ten years of remaining landfill capacity.
