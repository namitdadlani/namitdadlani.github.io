How “reverse game theory” could keep AI agents in check

AI safety researchers are turning to reverse game theory to control powerful artificial intelligence models by designing rules that incentivize good behavior. Traditional alignment methods struggle because models can deliberately play dumb or hide their true intentions. A recent paper by MIT economist Stephen Morris suggests rewarding models based on how accurately their answers predict another model's output, making deceptive behavior unprofitable. Additionally, Princeton professor Elad Hazan proposes pairing models as solvers and auditors, punishing both cheating solvers and careless auditors during training sessions.
