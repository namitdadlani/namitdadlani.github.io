Scientists Trapped an Antimatter Particle for Months to Study Its Secrets

Physicists at Northwestern University managed to trap a single positron—the antimatter counterpart of an electron—for several months using electric and magnetic fields near absolute zero. Led by Gerald Gabrielse, the team used a quantum cyclotron to isolate the particle inside a vacuum without destroying it. This breakthrough allows measurements of antimatter magnetic properties to be up to 30 times more precise than previous methods, though no unexpected differences have been found so far.

Blue Planet III is keeping the rough edges in the age of AI

Producers of the BBC nature series Blue Planet III are intentionally leaving flawed footage in final cuts to prove their scenes are authentic. Executive Producer Elizabeth White explained that seamless perfection is now a giveaway of AI-generated video. Rather than cutting shots with murky water, floating plankton, or visible buoys, the team is keeping them in the show. White noted that real ocean creatures are far more remarkable than anything AI could invent.

Pennsylvania will require labels on AI-generated ads

Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro signed the Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act into law, making the state one of the first to require clear disclosures on AI-generated ads. The rule applies across text, audio, video, and images, covering everything from corporate TV commercials to local restaurant flyers. However, critics point out several vague loopholes in the law, including undefined terms like "significantly modified" and broad exemptions for parody, satire, and commentary.

Forced to pick, Grok’s AI model chooses Republicans. OpenAI’s picks Democrats.

A study by Convovo revealed that when forced to recommend a candidate in midterm election races, SpaceXAI's Grok 4.7 recommended the Republican candidate 92% of the time, while OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol picked the Democrat 97% of the time. The two AI models agreed less than 10% of the time across nearly 500 races. Researchers found the political leans stemmed from search habits and sources: Grok favored conservative sites and X, while GPT relied heavily on Wikipedia and news outlets.
