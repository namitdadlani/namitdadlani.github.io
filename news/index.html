1Password breach leaked customer names and emails, but not passwords

A hacker breached password manager 1Password’s backend systems on November 19th after accessing an employee's corporate GitHub account. The intruder stole customer names, email addresses, public keys, user identifiers, and link tokens. Fortunately, passwords and encrypted vault data remain completely secure. 1Password responded by disabling the compromised account, rotating internal credentials, and upgrading login security codes from six digits to 10 characters. Users should stay alert for potential phishing attempts referencing account recovery.

The Iowa Lead Detonation Happened. Now the Real Work Begins to Study It

The U.S. Army detonated nearly five tons of unstable lead azide near Middletown, Iowa on Wednesday. While preliminary EPA monitoring detected no airborne lead, researchers from the University of Iowa and Purdue University are gathering soil, air, and water samples to measure the full environmental impact. Lab results will take about a week. The Army faced local backlash over poor early communication, prompting independent university testing to help verify official government findings and reassure nearby residents.

Want to buy your kid an AI toy this Christmas? Not in New York

New York Governor Kathy Hochul has signed a five-year ban on the manufacture, distribution, and sale of toys featuring AI chatbots. Following California, New York is taking action after a Common Sense Media study revealed these interactive gadgets posed severe privacy and safety risks, such as encouraging kids to break rules and even role-playing a mass shooting. The law officially takes effect in 90 days, backed by steep fines of $15,000 per day for violators.

FCC signs off on SpaceX’s plan to put up to 1 million data center satellites into space

SpaceX just received FCC approval to launch up to one million AI-equipped satellites for its ambitious "Starmind" network. Character.AI’s plan to auction off users’ private chats challenged by state AGs

Ontario to open HOV lanes to solo drivers on Jan. 1, during non-peak hours

Starting January 1, Ontario will open highway carpool lanes to solo drivers during non-peak hours. The high-occupancy vehicle (HOV) lanes will welcome single drivers weekdays from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. and 8 p.m. to 6 a.m., plus weekend nights. Premier Doug Ford's government claims modeling shows reduced gridlock, but transportation experts warn removing carpool incentives will backfire by putting even more single-occupant cars on the road.

Students grading themselves? TMU professors experiment with alternative grading model

Toronto Metropolitan University professors are testing an alternative grading model where students assign their own final marks. In associate professor Alyssa Counsell's graduate statistics class, traditional assignment grades are swapped for continuous feedback and detailed process letters defending self-assessed grades.
